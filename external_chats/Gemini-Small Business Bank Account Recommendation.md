As your fiduciary for this decision, I’ve analyzed your current banking footprint alongside the best options for a low-revenue LLC in Washington. Since you expect a few thousand dollars in revenue, the primary goal is fee avoidance and administrative simplicity.


Because you already have a personal ecosystem with Chase and Fidelity, you have two distinct paths: leverage existing relationships for "all-in-one" visibility, or use a "neobank" designed specifically for small LLCs.


1. The "Path of Least Resistance": Chase


Since you already bank with Chase, opening a Chase Business Complete Banking® account is the most convenient option.


The Cost: $15 monthly fee.

How to waive it: Maintain a $2,000 minimum daily balance. If your revenue stays in the account, this is easy. Alternatively, spend $2,000/month on a Chase Ink business card or receive $2,000 in deposits via Chase QuickAccept.

Why it works for you: You can see your business and personal balances in one app. Plus, as a Seattle resident, you have plenty of branches if you ever need to deposit cash or get a document notarized.


2. The "Modern & Free" Choice: Relay or Mercury


If you don't want to worry about maintaining a $2,000 balance, digital-first banks are superior for very small businesses.


Relay: Best for organization. It allows you to open up to 20 individual checking accounts with no fees or minimums. You could have one for "Taxes," one for "Operating Expenses," and one for "Owner Pay."

Mercury: Best for a "set it and forget it" tech-forward experience. It has $0 monthly fees, no minimums, and a very clean UI. Note: Mercury does not accept physical cash deposits, so it only works if your revenue is via ACH, wire, or Stripe/PayPal.


3. The "Yield" Play: Fidelity


You mentioned having personal accounts with Fidelity. While Fidelity is primarily a brokerage, they do offer business accounts.


Fidelity Business Checking: Usually has no monthly fees and no minimum balance.

The Benefit: If your LLC revenue sits idle, you can easily move it into a Money Market Fund (like SPAXX) within the same ecosystem to earn ~4-5% interest (current 2026 rates permitting) while keeping it liquid.

### Gemini

Beyond banking and accounting, there are several Washington-specific tax requirements and operational tools that will be important as you launch your Shopify and Etsy shops.


Washington State Tax & Licensing


Since you are operating in Seattle, you need to be aware of the "triple-threat" of local filing:


Business & Occupation (B&O) Tax: Washington does not have a personal income tax, but it does have a gross receipts tax. Even with small revenue, you must report this to the Department of Revenue (DOR). If your annual gross income is under $56,000, you likely won't owe any state B&O tax, but you still have to file a return to report $0 due.
+1

Washington State Business License: Since you have an LLC and plan to sell taxable goods, you must apply for a Washington State Business License through the DOR to get your Unified Business Identifier (UBI). This is what allows you to collect sales tax on Shopify.

Operating Agreement: While not legally required for a single-member LLC in Washington, it is highly recommended. It formally separates your personal assets from your business, which is essential to maintain the "liability shield" of your LLC.
+1


Sales Tax Management


Etsy: Etsy is a "Marketplace Facilitator." They will automatically collect and remit sales tax for your sales on their platform. You don't have to send that money to the state yourself, but you must report those "facilitated sales" on your excise tax return.
+2

Shopify: Unlike Etsy, Shopify is a tool, not a marketplace. You are responsible for setting up the tax rates and remitting the sales tax you collect from Washington customers to the DOR.
